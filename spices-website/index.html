    01 / 07 · The seed

    One seed, a twenty-year crop.

    Black pepper is the dried fruit of Piper nigrum, a perennial vine native to the Western Ghats of India. A new vine starts from seed or from a cutting of a proven mother plant, set at the foot of a support tree.

    Botanical name
    Piper nigrum L.
    Family
    Piperaceae
    Propagation
    Seed or rooted cutting
    02 / 07 · Sprouting

    The monsoon does the rest.

    Planting follows the first heavy rains. Warm, humid air and filtered shade bring up the first shoot and its heart-shaped leaves within weeks.

    Rainfall
    1,500–2,500 mm / year
    Optimal temp.
    20–30 °C
    Light
    Partial, filtered shade
    03 / 07 · The vine

    A climber that needs support.

    Pepper climbs by aerial roots that grip the bark of its support tree. It is trained to around six metres and comes into full bearing in its third or fourth year.

    First harvest
    Year 3
    Training height
    ≈ 6 m
    Productive life
    20+ years
    04 / 07 · Flowering

    Green spikes, ripening slowly.

    Flowers form on hanging spikes after the monsoon. Over six to eight months each spike fills with tightly packed berries that turn from green to red as they ripen.

    Spike length
    7–15 cm
    Fruit development
    6–8 months
    Ripening
    Green → yellow → red
    05 / 07 · Harvest

    Picked by hand, spike by spike.

    Harvest starts when one or two berries on a spike turn red. Pickers strip whole spikes into baskets, and the berries are then threshed from their stalks.

    Harvest signal
    1–2 red berries per spike
    Method
    Hand-picked
    Threshing
    Manual or mechanical
    06 / 07 · Sun drying

    A week in the sun.

    Berries are spread thin on clean mats and turned through the day. As moisture leaves, the skin darkens and wrinkles into the black peppercorn, with its full aroma and bite.

    Drying time
    7–10 days
    Target moisture
    ≤ 11 %
    Dry recovery
    ≈ 1/3 of fresh weight
    07 / 07 · Pack & ship
